🦴 NovaBone Dynamics

Bone-chain physics simulation add-on — turn hair, tails, skirts, accessories and jiggle bones into automatic physics animation with a few clicks.

Author: Chamiseul

🇺🇸 English | 🇰🇷 한국어

🆕 What's New in v1.3.0

Everything since v1.2.0 is listed here. The numbers are measured on test rigs (a chain over a sphere, a chain touching near its root, the same chain with a keyed root bone), each run from a clean cache.

Fix Bones: master bones are aimed, and only real animation blocks a bone

  • A bone whose child sits on its head is now aimed along that child. Unreal rigs pair a short master bone (MSTR_hair_back_r) with its first link at the very same joint. There is no gap to close and no direction to point at, so these bones used to be left alone, still pointing the way the exporter left them. They now follow the child's own axis, the line the chain actually runs along. Length does not change, and Fix Bones still checks that the skinned mesh did not move.
  • Only keys on a bone's own pose channels (location, rotation, scale) block it. A keyed custom property or constraint influence does not move the mesh when the bone is re-aimed, but it used to mark the whole bone as animated.
  • Check lists the bones it left alone because of keys, by name, instead of only counting them.

Hair roots buried in the character no longer throw the chain around

On a character the collision target is usually the body itself, and a hair root sits inside the scalp, an earring stud inside the ear. That part of the chain was put there by the animation; the solver treated it as a collision and pushed it out about a root that cannot move, which threw the whole chain around.

The part of the chain that the animated pose already buries in the collider, counted unbroken from the chain's root, is now left alone. A bone that the animation swings through the collider from the middle of the chain is still a collision. Meshes that are not closed volumes (a plane, a cloth) have no inside and are never treated as buried.

Measured with the root of a chain 0.14 m inside a sphere (distance of the tip from its animated position, before → after): static root 0.395 → 0.087 m, moving root 0.570 → 0.147 m, soft chain 0.589 → 0.186 m, sharpest joint 89° → 28° with the root moving. A chain buried deeper (half of it inside) stays on its animation as before (0.040 → 0.047 m).

💡 If the simulation still goes wild with the whole character as the target, see Troubleshooting — "A character as the collision target".

The first bone no longer swings hard around its fixed head

The chain's first bone hangs from an animated parent, so its head cannot move. When a collider sat closer to that head than Radius, every contact pushed the bone out to the full Radius about a pivot that stayed put — the "ぐきぃ" kink right below the root. The distance a bone has to keep now grows from the head's real gap up to the full Radius at the tip, so the bone leans away just enough.

Measured over 12 settings (3 Radius × 2 Stiffness × static / moving root) at 4 collider positions each: the first bone's deviation from its animated direction 57° → 16°, the sharpest joint 58° → 16°, jerk 0.065 → 0.011, chatter 0.0225 → 0.0161, penetration 0 before and after. No setting got worse. Probe mode only — Precise keeps the exact full-radius contact.

Influence below 1 no longer lets the pose into the collider

Influence mixes the simulated pose with the raw animation, and the animation knows nothing about the collider, so a mix could end up inside it. The mixed pose is now pushed out again, and the bones below it follow, so the chain stays joined. Penetration on a chain pulled through a sphere: Influence 0.5 0.062 → 0, Influence 0.3 0.098 → 0.

New: Tip Radius

Bone Collision Shape → Tip Radius. The collision radius of the last bone as a fraction of Radius; bones in between taper linearly. 1 (default) keeps the same radius all along. The bone shapes drawn by Show Bone Shapes follow it.

New: Keep Animated Clearance

Bone Collision Shape → Keep Animated Clearance (off by default). Where the animated pose already sits closer to the collider than Radius — hair resting on a scalp — the bone keeps only that gap instead of being pushed out to the full Radius. The allowed gap drops at once and grows back at 40% a frame, and never goes below 30% of Radius, so the bone cannot sink into the surface. Measured: tip distance from the animated pose 0.054 → 0.042 (−23%), jerk 0.027 → 0.017 (−35%), penetration 0.

Stiffness now works in Chain mode

In Chain mode the Stiffness pull was computed and then thrown away, so Stiffness did nothing — 0, 0.3 and 0.6 gave the exact same motion. Head Stiffness (Floating Head) had the same bug and never pulled the head back. Both work now.

⚠️ Existing scenes will look stiffer. Every Chain-mode scene and preset was tuned with no Stiffness at all. If a chain now holds itself up too much, lower Stiffness: around 0.05–0.1 drapes, 0.5 already holds a chain nearly straight against gravity.

Thin chains no longer sink into colliders

A probe deeper inside the collider than its own radius was not detected, so once a point got that deep it kept sinking. A point behind the surface is now detected (confirmed by a ray through the back of the face, so a point outside a sharp edge is not mistaken for one inside).

Penetration depth with Radius 0.01: chain over a sphere 0.028 → 0.006 (worst 0.073 → 0.018), the same chain with a moving root 0.089 → 0.0003, Suzanne 0.196 → 0.008, cube 0.011 → 0.0004. A normal Radius is unchanged.

Contacts near a bone's root no longer kink it

When a collider touched a bone near its head, only the tail was pushed. The bone swung hard around its head while the parent did not move, so the chain kinked right below the touched joint. The push is now shared: the tail takes the part for where along the bone the contact is, and the parent's tail (this bone's head) takes the rest.

Measured over 7 scenes (5 collider positions each): jerk lower in six and level in one — chain with a moving root and Radius 0.01 0.166 → 0.035, the same chain at rest 0.123 → 0.035, collider at a bone's head 0.176 → 0.093; collider near the root, sharpest joint 29° → 16°. Chatter stays under 0.04 everywhere.

Bones land exactly on the simulated chain

Inside the solver each bone kept measuring from where its parent's tail used to be after a collision or smoothing step moved it, so the chain came apart at the joints and the bones landed 9–18 mm off the simulated shape. Gap is now 0.

Rebuilding a chain no longer moves its rest pose

Adding or removing a chain, Copy Settings or Undo rebuilds the simulation. The rebuild took the pose the physics had left on the bones as the new rest pose, so the Stiffness goal crept further each time. The rest pose is now kept across rebuilds. Capture Current Pose As Base still sets a new one on purpose.

Keyframes are read correctly on Blender 4.4+

Keyframes on 4.4+ actions (layers / slots) were not found, so an animated parent bone in a chain was treated as unanimated.

Simulation Nodes engine

  • No jump on the first played frame — the obstacles are now set up before the pre-roll, so the strand settles against them, and the bones are posed on the settled strand at Build. Frame 1 → 2 jump: 0.76 m → 0.008 m.
  • Mirrored chains move identically — left/right difference 0.58 m → 0.00002 m.
  • Bones follow the strand exactly — 0.53 m → 0.005 m.
  • Obstacle sliders update live — Margin, Friction, Softness, Deforming Target and Edge Contacts no longer wait for a rebuild.
  • Obstacle Friction is the panel's Friction — it used to read the hidden NovaSol Friction slider instead.
  • Time Scale now also slows down / speeds up the strand.

💡 One obstacle mesh carries one Collider setup, so when two chains share an obstacle, the chain built last sets its Margin / Friction.

✨ Features

  • ✅ Pick a chain's start & end — simulation just works
  • ✅ Scale-independent: identical motion on 1.0 Blender rigs and 0.01 Unreal-import rigs
  • ✅ Tune collision visually with live shaper overlays
  • ✅ Shape-accurate collision: Capsule / Cylinder / Box are probed as real volumes (not just visuals) with multi-contact resolution and sweep CCD anti-tunnelling
  • ✅ Stable contacts: anti-fold (no bone ever snaps inside-out) and anti-kink (draped chains relax smoothly instead of zig-zagging)
  • ✅ Chain mode + Spring (jiggle) mode
  • ✅ Works as a collider for Blender cloth & hair sims
  • ✅ Bake to keyframes for game-engine export

📥 Installation

  1. Select the files inside the novabone folder and compress them to a zip

    ⚠️ Do NOT compress the folder itself — blender_manifest.toml must be at the zip root.

  2. Blender → Edit > Preferences > Get Extensions
  3. Top-right ▼ → Install from Disk → pick the zip
  4. Enable NovaBone
  5. In the 3D viewport press N → a NovaBone tab appears

Supported: Blender 4.2+ (tested on 4.5 LTS, 5.0, 5.2 LTS)

🚀 Quick Start (5 minutes)

▲ Select bones → create chains → play. That's it.

  1. Select the armature, enter Pose Mode
  2. Select the bones you want to simulate (for a connected tail: click the ROOT bone last, Shift+click the rest)
  3. N-panel → Setup Tools → Chains from Selection
  4. The bones sag slightly — success! Press Spacebar to play
  5. Move a body bone — the chain follows with physics 🎉

💡 Shake & Play kicks the bones AND starts playback automatically. If they jiggle, the engine is alive.

🎯 Collision precision engine (v1.0.0)

NovaSol's collision is no longer a single sphere per bone. Every substep:

  1. Volume probing — the chain's chosen Shape (Capsule / Cylinder / Box) is sampled as a bounded set of sphere probes laid through its REAL volume: spine points for capsules, lateral rings for cylinders, corner offsets for boxes. Mid-bone leaks through walls and "Box pushes round" are gone.
  2. Multi-contact — up to 3 DISTINCT contacts (normals > 60° apart): the deepest at full strength, the others at 60%, so a bone resting across a corner seats on BOTH walls instead of rattling on one.
  3. Sweep CCD — a bone that moved more than half its collision radius in one substep gets its PATH tested and is snapped back to the last free station. Fast whips no longer tunnel through thin plates.
  4. Anti-fold — every contact correction is budgeted (max 75% of the bone's length per round) and direction-clamped (max 45° per round), so a bone can never snap folded inside-out; it slides around the surface over a few frames instead.
  5. Anti-kink — after contacts resolve, every bone relaxes 15% toward its neighbours' average direction (Chain mode only), which smooths the zig-zag — and the hairpins — a draped chain would otherwise keep. Two exemptions, both about WHERE it may act: a bone in contact is left where the collision put it, and the chain's end bones are left alone. Both are lifted when the joint is a real FOLD (past 90°), because otherwise a bone folded back on its neighbour had no pass that could touch it — and because the TRANSIENT V a seating chain forms around first contact peaks at 96-99°, which the old 100°/120° thresholds watched happen without acting. Measured on a 10-bone drape over a sphere: worst joint 176° with the old rule, 152° with the 120° threshold, 87° with this one; the contact-zone kink on a settling chain (measured on a user scene) peaks at 98.8° → 31°. A scene that is not folding is unchanged (harness sphere: tip chatter 0.336 → 0.339, rest height 0.4355 → 0.4338).

If a chain coils hard and this rule is not enough, relaxing the ends at every angle flattens it completely (measured: worst joint 19° instead of 152°), at the cost of re-shaping ordinary drapes as well (harness sphere rest height 0.4355 → 0.2651). That trade is why it is not the default. 6. Residual re-check — any bone whose round was clamped or truncated gets one extra query + a small outward push so the round always ends OUTSIDE the surface. Clean frames pay none of this cost. 7. Inside detection — a probe that is already behind the surface, by up to half the bone's length beyond its radius, is pushed back out. A ray along the face normal has to leave through the back of a face, so a point outside a sharp edge is not mistaken for one inside. 8. Shared push — a contact near a bone's head moves the parent's tail too, in proportion to how close to the head it is, so the bone does not swing hard around its head. The first bone's head is pinned to its parent and takes the whole push itself. 9. Root relief — for that first bone, a head that already sits closer than the radius is not asked to keep the full radius: the distance the probes must keep grows from the head's real gap to the full radius at the tip (Probe mode). 10. Buried parts left alone — the part of the chain that the animation already buries in a closed collider, unbroken from the chain's root, is not treated as a collision.

💡 All of this is automatic — no new sliders. If the panel's Solve xx ms climbs, lower Substeps first; probe density already covers what extra substeps used to.

Contact Mode: Probe (Fast) vs Precise (Mesh) — v1.1.0

Everything above describes Probe (Fast), the default: the bone's volume is sampled at up to 16 spheres per bone per substep. A sample can only report a contact once its own sphere has reached the surface, so a small feature sitting between two samples is under-measured.

Precise (Mesh) replaces that measurement. A triangulated capsule proxy is intersected with the collider's BVH (BVHTree.overlap, a C-side traversal) and each candidate triangle is then measured against the bone's real segment, so the reported depth is exact and the path cannot leak between samples.

Probe (Fast) Precise (Mesh)
Contact depth sampled exact
Small feature between two probes under-measured (measured 0.0086 = ~9% on a 0.1 unit plate) exact (0.0000)
Solve cost 1x measured 3.0x (Capsule), 2.0x (Box), 1.8x (Cylinder) on a 6-bone chain — pair it with Substeps 8
Collision solid the chain's Shape (Capsule / Cylinder / Box) the bone's capsule, with the Shape's own probe depth kept as a floor

⚠️ About that last row: Precise measures the bone's capsule exactly, but it never pushes LESS than Probe did. For every face the probes already saw it keeps the larger of the two depths, so switching a chain to Precise cannot make it sink deeper than it used to — verified on a Cylinder chain approaching laterally, where the capsule alone came out 0.011 shallower at Radius 0.25 (the shape's lateral probes model a surface up to 1.8x the radius). The exact measurement only ever adds reach where the probes had under-measured.

Measured on the same 6-bone sphere scene: rest height is preserved (Cylinder 0.3753 Probe → 0.3753 Precise, Box 0.3734 → 0.3744) while tip chatter improves (Cylinder 0.348 → 0.277, Box 0.366 → 0.277). Use Precise where walls are thin or edges are sharp — not as a way to make a Box behave like a box.

Show Bone Shapes ⭐

▲ Capsules span each bone head-to-tail and move with it. Raising Radius grows the diameter

▲ Capsules around a hair chain next to the collision target

Shapers are NOT new bones — they visualize each bone's collision volume.

  • Choose Capsule / Cylinder / Box
  • Radius = collision thickness (ratio to bone length — scale-free)
  • Pixel-locked to the simulated bones during playback
  • In Legacy Physics (Collision) engine mode, shapers ARE the real collision objects
  • In NovaSol mode the chosen shape now also drives the ACTUAL collision volume (see Collision precision engine above)

💡 Tuning: Show Bone Shapes ON → adjust Radius → where the capsule slightly overlaps the body is where collision begins.

📋 Recipes

👱 Hair 1. Select hair bones (root last) → `Chains from Selection` 2. Preset: **Hair** 3. Show Bone Shapes ON → adjust Radius 4. Collision: NovaSol + body mesh, **Influence 1.0**, Bounce 0.1, Margin 0.03 5. Play — the hair should slide off the arm
👝 Multiple accessories 1. Select all accessory bones → `Chains from Selection` (Auto) 2. Tune one → **Copy Active Chain Settings** 3. Use each row's 🎯 select button to find which bones belong to which chain
🎒 Spring bag 1. Create the bag chain → Preset: **Spring** 2. Shake & Play → check the bounce 3. Raise Damping to settle faster
🩳 Skirt 1. `Chains from Prefix` if you use one 2. Preset: **Cloth** 3. Cone Limit 60–70° 4. Self Collision ON (prevents panel overlap)
🧵 Cloth integration 1. Engine = **Legacy Physics (Collision)**, Profile = **Cloth** 2. **Show Bone Shapes ON** 3. Cloth modifier on the garment → check **Cloth Collisions** 4. Play
⛓️ Draping a chain over a sphere/body (contact tuning) *▲ A chain falls onto a sphere and drapes over it (Cloth preset, Stiffness 0)* 1. Influence **1.0**, Stretch **0**, Stiffness around **0.05–0.1** 2. Bounce **0.05–0.15**, Friction **0.4–0.6**, Margin **0.03** 3. Radius 0.18–0.25, Substeps 12 4. Play — the chain should drape and slide, no folding, no kinks

🔧 Troubleshooting

Physics doesn't move at all 1. NovaBone Enabled / Enable Armature are ON 2. Freeze is OFF 3. **Is playback running?** (paused = physics paused — by design) 4. Click Shake & Play — if it jiggles, the engine is fine 5. **Stiffness too high?** From 0.5 up a chain holds its base pose almost rigidly. Lower it (0.05–0.1 for hair that should hang) 6. Diagnostics → check the System Console
A character as the collision target 1. **Do not use the whole character.** Duplicate the head and body into a plain collider mesh and delete everything else (hair cards, earrings, clothes that follow the chain bones). Parts of the target that are **skinned to the simulated bones** move with them, so the chain can end up colliding with geometry it is itself dragging around. 2. Put the collider copy in **Collide With**. 3. Hair roots buried in the scalp are fine now. If a chain root sits right on the surface instead, **Radius** × bone length is the gap it keeps: lower Radius, or turn **Keep Animated Clearance** on. 4. A head that deforms (face bones, shape keys) needs no extra setting; the moving surface is measured where it is touched. 5. Test the collider copy first: if the simulation settles, the original target had geometry skinned to the chain.
NovaSol collision doesn't block 1. Enable Collision ON + body mesh assigned 2. **Sim Influence at 1.0** — at lower values the visible pose is blended back toward the raw animation and then pushed out again, which is less exact than the simulation itself 3. Radius 0.2–0.3, Margin 0.02–0.05 4. Check for red `Collider failed: name` at the panel bottom 5. Fast motion → Substeps 10–16 (sweep CCD also engages automatically)
A few bones fold / collapse at the contact zone 1. Update to the v1.0.0 solver (anti-fold + anti-kink built in) 2. Bounce down to 0.1, Friction to 0.4–0.6 3. Substeps 10–16 — shallower per-step sinks mean the fold guards rarely engage and motion stays smooth 4. Reset Physics after changing settings
Cloth doesn't collide 1. Show Bone Shapes ON 2. Check **Cloth Collisions** on the cloth modifier 3. Engine = Legacy Physics (Collision) + Profile = Cloth
Bones snap / drift away 1. **Remove constraints (IK etc.) from simulated bones** 2. Reset Physics 3. Avoid non-uniform object scale
Fix Bones (orientation) crashes or does nothing 1. Bones whose child sits exactly ON the bone's head (Unreal `MSTR_x` / `x_1` pairs) are aimed along that child. Check reports them as "child-on-head (aimed along the child)" 2. Use **Check** first — it lists how many bones are off, and names the bones it left alone because they have keys on location / rotation / scale 3. A bone with such keys is skipped on purpose: re-aiming it would change what its keys do to the mesh. **Leave Animated Bones Alone** off lifts that, and can fold the character up 4. **Use NovaBone Chain Bones** on means the name pattern is ignored

❓ FAQ

Q. What are the NovaShape_ objects?* Collision-volume visualizations. They're removed when Show Bone Shapes is turned off and always hidden from renders.

Q. How do I get this into a game engine? Bake NovaBone to Keyframes, then export FBX. Game engines replay the baked animation.

Q. Performance with the new collision? Volume probing is bounded (≤ ~16 sphere queries per bone per target, multi-contact and CCD only cost extra ON contact frames). Watch the per-chain solve time (ms) at the panel bottom; if it climbs, lower Substeps — probe density already covers what extra substeps used to.

Q. Single-bone accessories? Yes — Chains from Selection with exactly one bone now creates a 1-bone chain directly. Or use Individual mode, or enable Include Single Bones in Detect Chains.

Q. Which chain owns which bones? Press the 🎯 button on that chain's row — its bones light up in the viewport and it becomes the active chain.
